Our Philosophy

Restraint is the hardest discipline.

We cook within forty miles and within the week — langoustine landed at Eyemouth before dawn, roots pulled from clay we have walked ourselves, herbs cut minutes rather than days ago.

Chef Élodie Vasquez builds each menu around a single ingredient at its brief peak, then removes everything that would distract from it. What remains is quiet, precise and unmistakably of this place: three elements on a plate, each one earned. Craft here is measured in what we choose to leave out.

É. Vasquez Chef-Patron
I

Grown Nearby

Eleven growers, four boats and one small dairy supply nearly everything we serve. We buy the whole harvest, not the prettiest tenth of it.

II

Written Daily

The menu is set at ten each morning and printed at four. If a delivery disappoints, the dish does not appear — there is no substitute waiting in the walk-in.

III

Made by Hand

Butter churned in-house, vinegars aged three years in the cellar, bread proved overnight in the same oak trough since we opened.

The Kitchen

Two Hands, One Pass

A brigade of nine, unchanged for four years. Everyone who cooks your dinner also serves part of it.

ÉV

Élodie Vasquez

Chef-Patron

Raised between Biarritz and the Cairngorms, Élodie trained under Pascal Ferrand in Lyon before spending six years on the fish station at Sørvind in Bergen. She opened Maison Solenne in a former corn merchant's hall in 2014 with eleven seats and one commis. She still writes every menu by hand at the corner table before service.

Two stars since 2019 · Nordic Chef of the Year, 2021
TO

Tomás Oyelaran

Head Pastry Chef & Fermentation

Tomás arrived for a three-month stage in 2016 and never left. He keeps the cellar — some two hundred crocks of vinegars, garums and preserved fruit — and treats dessert as the final argument of the meal rather than its apology. His barley cream has not changed in seven years, and never will.

Pastry Talent of the Year, Guild of Chefs, 2023
