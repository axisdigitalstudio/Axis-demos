How a Scent Unfolds

Top, heart, base.

The order a fragrance actually reveals itself in — not marketing language, the real sequence, measured in minutes on skin.

Top0–15 min

The introduction. Citrus peel, aldehydes, or green leaf — gone fastest, felt first.

Heart15 min–3 hrs

The actual character of the scent. Rose, iris, fig leaf — what you'll be known for wearing.

Base3–10 hrs

What's left on a collar the next morning. Oud, amber, cedar, leather — the slowest, heaviest molecules.

The Atelier

Built by one nose, not a lab.

NOCTURNE has one perfumer, no fragrance-house committee, and no focus group. Every formula is hers from first sketch to final blend, mixed in a single room above the workshop in Grasse.

Materials are sourced directly from growers we've worked with for over a decade — not brokers. When a harvest fails, the scent that depends on it pauses. It does not get diluted to keep shipping.

I

Sourced, Not Substituted

Every rare material comes from a named grower. If the harvest is thin, the batch is smaller — the formula never changes to compensate.

II

Numbered by Batch

Each bottle carries a batch number tied to the exact week it was blended. No two batches are chemically identical, and we don't pretend otherwise.

III

Retired, Not Reformulated

When a scent's defining material runs out for good, the scent ends. We've never rebuilt one around a substitute ingredient.
