VAT is monthly, and it runs two months behind

Owners who have traded in Europe or the Gulf assume a quarterly return. In Egypt it is monthly — and the deadline is the end of the second month after the period. January's return is due 31 March. That lag is why a company can look compliant in March while two months of unreconciled sales sit underneath.

April is the exception nobody remembers

The April return does not follow the rule. It is due by 15 June, two weeks early, so that the state's own year closes clean. It is the single most commonly missed date in the Egyptian calendar, and the penalty runs from the fifteenth, not the thirtieth.

Form 41 is due even when it is empty

The quarterly withholding return covers tax deducted from local suppliers — typically 0.5% to 5% on payments above 300 EGP. If you withheld nothing that quarter, you still file. A nil return is a filing, not a non-event, and the fine for silence is the same as for lateness.

The 15th is two obligations, not one

Salary tax and social insurance contributions both fall due in the first half of the following month, but they go to two different authorities with two different reference numbers. Paying one on time and the other late is the most common payroll error we inherit.

The return follows the audit, not the calendar

The corporate return is due four months after year end — 30 April for a December year end. But it cannot be filed until the financial statements are signed by a licensed auditor, and an auditor cannot sign in a fortnight. Work that starts in April finishes late.

Your signature token expires

The e-invoicing certificate on the USB token has a fixed life. When it lapses your company cannot issue a valid invoice at all — not late, not at all — and renewal takes days, not hours. We diarise it a month out for every client we onboard.

This grid describes a company with a 31 December financial year and no special regime. Free zone companies, investment-law entities, simplified-regime microbusinesses and anyone with a non-calendar year end sit on a different clock. Deadlines that land on an official holiday move to the next working day. Ask us before you rely on it.

Questions

The five things people ask before they call.

Money and trust, mostly. Answered here so you do not have to sit through a meeting to find out.

What will this actually cost me?

A company with one bank account, under 150 transactions a month and eight employees pays about 7,100 EGP a month — bookkeeping and VAT at 4,500, payroll at 2,000 plus 600 for the headcount — and 22,000 EGP once a year for the accounts and corporate return. Quarterly filings are 1,600 EGP each.

That is the whole fee. We do not bill for calls, emails, bank letters or the ten-minute questions. If your volumes fall outside the band, we tell you before the work starts, not after.

Do you handle e-invoicing, including setting it up from scratch?

Yes, both. If you are not yet on the ETA system we register the company, obtain the digital signature token, map your products to EGS or GS1 codes, connect whatever you invoice from — ERP, an accounting package or a spreadsheet — and train the person who raises invoices. Setup is 9,500 EGP and usually takes two weeks, most of it waiting on the certificate.

If you are already integrated, we monitor submissions, chase rejected documents, and check that your suppliers' invoices actually reached the portal. Input tax you cannot trace to a valid electronic invoice is input tax you will lose at inspection.

My books are a mess and I am behind on filings. Will you take me on?

Yes. Roughly a third of new clients arrive with a backlog, and a fair number arrive with an assessment already issued. We do not lecture about it.

What we do is reconstruct the position first: what was filed, what was not, and what the exposure is with penalties and delay interest. You get that in writing with a fixed price to clear it before you commit to a retainer. Clearing a year of neglected books typically runs 15,000 to 60,000 EGP depending on volume — and in most cases it is cheaper than the penalties that accrue while you decide.

Are you actually licensed, and can I check?

Yes, and yes. The practice is entered on the Register of Accountants and Auditors under number 3184, and Rania Nassar holds the individual audit licence under that entry — only a licensed auditor may sign statutory financial statements in Egypt. Tarek Halim is an accredited tax agent, ETA register 11-4470. Both registrations are public and verifiable at the relevant authority.

We also carry professional indemnity insurance, and we will show you the certificate before you sign anything.

What happens if I get audited?

We take it. You forward the notice and stop replying to the inspector yourself — most damage in an Egyptian inspection is done by informal answers given early.

We assemble the file, respond in writing, attend the internal committee with you or in your place under power of attorney, and negotiate the assessment. If it remains wrong, we take it to the appeal committee. For retainer clients this is included up to the internal committee stage; appeal work is quoted separately, usually from 25,000 EGP.

Between 2019 and 2025 we defended thirty-one inspections. We will tell you honestly which parts of a claim are arguable and which are not — a defence built on a weak position costs more than the tax.
