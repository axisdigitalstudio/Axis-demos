The signature

Springs aren't difficulty settings.

Most people assume more springs means a harder class. It's the opposite as often as not — heavy springs support you, light springs make you do the work. Here's what each one is actually for.

Your instructor will call these by colour during class. Now you'll know why.

Red — full tension

Heaviest. Used for footwork and pressing, where it carries weight so your joints don't.

Supports
Blue — three quarters

The everyday spring. Enough resistance to build strength without losing control of the movement.

Builds
Yellow — half

Arms, shoulders, anything above the ribcage. Light enough that form fails before the muscle does.

Refines
Green — quarter

Almost nothing to push against, which is exactly the point. This is where stability gets tested.

What the first class is like

Arrive ten minutes early. You'll be shown the machine before anyone touches it, and the instructor will ask about injuries and anything a doctor has told you.

  • Wear anything you can move in — leggings and a fitted top
  • Grip socks are required; your first pair is on us
  • Don't eat in the hour before
  • You will be sore in places you didn't expect
