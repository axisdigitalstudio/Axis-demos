project is put together

Two drawings do most of the talking in our office: the plan, which sets how you move, and the wall section, which sets how the building feels and how long it lasts. Here is one of each, at real proportion.

A-101 — Ground plan Ridgeline House · 3,240 sq ft

0 — 10 ft  ·  North ↑ up

Great room

The only double-height volume. Its west wall is the board-formed core, which absorbs afternoon heat and releases it after sundown.

A-501 — Wall section Typical exterior · 397 mm

  • Board-formed concreteStructure + thermal mass200mm

Why it matters: the insulation sits outside the concrete, not inside it. That single move lets the slab work as a thermal battery, removes every cold bridge at the floor edge, and is the reason a Meridian house holds temperature overnight with the mechanical system off.

The studio

Twelve people,
one drawing board

Meridian Atelier was founded in Sonoma in 2011 by Ilse Navarro and Töm Rask. We take six to eight projects a year — enough that a partner is on every site visit, few enough that we still draw by hand first.

A building should be legible. You should be able to read how it stands up just by looking at it.

14yrsIn practice
68Built projects
9Design awards
12People in studio
  1. 01

    Structure stays visible

    If a beam is doing work, you should see it doing the work. We don't box out structure to make a room look calmer — we size it so it already is.

  2. 02

    Materials must age well

    Unlacquered brass darkens, oak silvers, lime plaster chalks. We specify finishes that are better in year ten than in year one — and refuse the ones that aren't.

  3. 03

    Draw the section first

    Plans get you through a building; sections tell you how it feels once you're inside. Every Meridian project begins with a hand section at 1:20.
